# Options Setup: what the options market is pricing (`/options`)

**The question:** on the AI stocks and the indexes, what do the listed options say: how much movement is priced (implied against realised volatility), how it is priced across time (the VIX term structure), how it is priced across strikes (skew, put/call positioning, dealer gamma), how big a move each expiry allows for against the event calendar, and what a given structure would pay.

Six tabs. Data at `/api/options/<setup|chains|vol|skew|moves|lab>`, one symbol's chain at `/api/options/chain-<SYM>`. Started 8 Nov 2026 from the owner's request: "Set up a stock option setup dashboard". A trading portal, if one comes, is a separate dashboard; this one is read-only analysis.

**Not investment advice.** The page describes what the options market implies and makes no buy or sell call. The Strategy lab is a calculator: it prices structures from the delayed chain and a model, trades nothing and stores nothing.

## Method: how each figure is calculated

**Trade date.** The chain's session is the date of the quote's `last_trade_time`, not the file's `timestamp` (Cboe writes the file through the night). Outside market hours the page shows the last session's chain and says so.

**The front month** is the nearest standard monthly expiry (third Friday, or the Thursday before it when the Friday is a holiday) at least 5 days out, so the week of expiration does not stand for the month; when none is listed, the nearest expiry at least 5 days out. **The next monthly** (the expected-move column on Setup) is the same expiry.

**ATM IV** per expiry: the mean of the call and put implied volatility at the strike nearest spot (one alone if the other has no quote). Cboe's own `iv30` is shown beside it on the Volatility tab as a check.

**Term structure**: ATM IV by days to expiry across every listed expiry; "a week ago" is the same from the daily entry at least six days old.

**IV rank** = (today's front ATM IV − the lowest in the history) ÷ (the highest − the lowest) × 100, over up to a year (252 entries) of the accrued history. **IV percentile** = the share of those days on which IV was lower than today. Both need history, which this dashboard writes itself: one entry per trading day from the day it first ran. The page says how many days exist. **A stock's own rank and percentile are not shown, and the `ai_iv_rank` signal carries no value and cannot cross, until it has 10 days of history (`MIN_RANK_DAYS`)**: over a day or two the rank is 0 or 100 by construction, which is not a reading; the signal row says "needs 10 days of IV history (n so far)" meanwhile. Until 60 exist (`MIN_HISTORY`): the index ETFs (SPY, QQQ, IWM, SMH) show the **VIX's** rank and percentile over its last year instead, marked "VIX"; a stock shows its own thin figures marked with the day count. The **AI basket's median IV rank** is the median over the core 13 of whatever each one has, with the thin ones named.

**Expected move** to an expiry, two ways, both shown: (1) **straddle**: the ATM call mid + the ATM put mid, as a share of spot, what the market charges for the move in either direction (about 0.8 standard deviations when prices are lognormal); (2) **IV**: spot × ATM IV × √(days ÷ 365), one standard deviation, about a 68% band if returns were normal. The cone on Expected moves draws both around spot to each listed expiry; the event calendar is laid over it.

**Expected against realised** (Expected moves, bottom): for each daily entry and each of its expiries that has passed, the IV expected move that day against the move from that day's spot to the close at expiry (the last close on or before the expiry date); "inside" when the realised move is within one standard deviation. About 68% should land inside if options price movement fairly; more means options were rich, fewer means they were cheap. Fills in as expiries pass.

**25-delta skew** per expiry = the IV of the 25-delta put − the IV of the 25-delta call, each by linear interpolation in |delta| across that side's strikes (none when 0.25 is outside the quoted range), in volatility points. Higher = the market pays more for downside than upside. The **risk reversal** is the same number with the opposite sign (call IV − put IV). The basket's percentile is against its own accrued history once 60 days exist; until then it is read off the **Cboe SKEW index's** percentile over its last year (the SKEW index measures the same thing for the S&P 500, on a different scale).

**Put/call ratios** by the day's volume and by open interest, over every listed expiry within 400 days and strikes 60-150% of spot (the part of the chain the page keeps); also per expiry.

**Max pain** per expiry: the strike S at which the expiry's open interest would be worth least if the stock settled there, argmin over strikes of Σ calls max(0, S − K) × OI + Σ puts max(0, K − S) × OI. A description of where open interest sits, not a target.

**Dealer gamma exposure (GEX)** per strike = gamma × open interest × 100 × spot² × 1%, in dollars per 1% move in the underlying, with **calls positive and puts negative**: the convention that market makers are long the calls customers sold (overwriting) and short the puts customers bought (protection). A positive total means dealers hedge against the move (buy dips, sell rips: dampening); negative means they hedge with it (amplifying). The convention is an assumption about who holds what, not a fact about any book, and open interest is the previous session's; the figure says nothing about who is long or short any line. Per expiry on Chains, the total over all kept expiries on Setup and Skew & flow.

**Realised volatility**: the annualised standard deviation of daily log returns over the last 10 and 30 trading days (× √252), from the closes. **The variance risk premium** here is front-month ATM IV − 30-day realised, in volatility points, and the **VRP share** is the share of symbols on which it is positive. Positive is the normal state (options carry a premium to the movement seen); negative means the market moved more than options priced.

**Unusual activity**: lines with the day's volume more than 5 times their open interest and at least 1,000 contracts, sorted by that ratio: positions opened that day rather than rolled. The "premium traded" column is volume × mid × 100, an upper bound at the mid. It does not say whether the lines were bought or sold, or by whom.

**The Strategy lab** prices a structure (long call or put, bull call or bear put vertical, straddle, strangle, iron condor, covered call, cash-secured put) from the chain: each leg at its bid-ask mid, the last trade where there is no two-sided quote, a Black-Scholes price at the expiry's ATM IV where there is neither (the table says which). Profit and loss at expiry over 0 to 5× spot; **max profit** and **max loss** from that range, "unlimited" on a side that keeps growing (net long calls above every strike, or net short calls); **breakevens** where the expiry line crosses zero; the **dated** line is Black-Scholes on the chosen day holding each leg's IV and the chosen risk-free rate (default 4%). **Probability of profit** is the chance, under a lognormal model with the legs' mean IV and the risk-free rate as drift, that the price at expiry lands where the expiry line is above zero (the sum of N(d2) differences between breakevens). Commissions, assignment, early exercise and dividends are left out. A calculator, computed in the browser, nothing stored, nothing traded.

## Readings and signals: what +1 and −1 mean

Leans: +1 = calmer pricing, −1 = stress. Readings (`READINGS()`, from cached payloads): `vix_term` (−1 inverted, +1 above +2 points), `ai_iv_rank` (−1 above 80, +1 below 20), `vrp_share` (−1 below 30%, +1 above 70%), `ai_skew` (−1 above 8 points, +1 below 2), `spy_pc_oi` (−1 above 2.0, +1 below 1.2), `spy_em` (context, lean 0).

Signals proposed on 8 Nov 2026 (`data/signals/watchlist.json`, `"options"`, marked `proposed` until the owner approves): `vix_term` VIX3M − VIX, watch below 0 or above +5; `ai_iv_rank` the basket's median IV rank, watch above 80 or below 20 (no value, never crossed, until 10 days of history); `skew_ai` the basket's median 25-delta skew, watch above the 90th or below the 10th percentile of its own history (the SKEW index's percentile until 60 days exist); `vrp` the share of symbols with front IV above 30-day realised, watch below 30% or above 90%; `pc_oi` SPY's put/call open-interest ratio, watch above 2.0 or below 1.0.

## Caveats

- **Delayed quotes.** Cboe's feed is 15 minutes behind, and outside market hours the chain is the last session's close: the page is a reading of the setup, not a live screen. The file Cboe writes overnight can carry an earlier `last_trade_time` than the calendar date; the trade date on the page is that time's date.
- **History accrues slowly.** IV rank, IV percentile, the skew percentile, IV-vs-realised over time and expected-vs-realised all come from this dashboard's own daily entries, so they are thin for the first two months and empty for the first ten trading days (no rank is shown or signalled before then). The fallbacks (the VIX's percentile for the index ETFs, the SKEW index's for the basket's skew) are substitutes, not the same measure. Nothing is back-filled: there is no free source of historical implied volatility per stock.
- **GEX is a convention.** The dealer sign convention (long calls, short puts) is the common one and often wrong for a given line; open interest is a day old; the figure is one input to how a move might be hedged, not a prediction. Gamma here is Cboe's, under its own model.
- **Expected moves are prices, not forecasts.** The straddle and the IV move are what the market charges for movement; realised moves land outside the one-standard-deviation band about a third of the time by construction, and more often than that in fat-tailed markets.
- **The probability of profit assumes lognormal returns** at the legs' mean implied volatility with the risk-free rate as drift: a model number, not a forecast; real distributions are fatter-tailed and skewed, which the skew figures on this very page show.
- **Thin lines.** Deep out-of-the-money options at a few cents produce extreme vol/OI ratios and noisy IVs; the unusual-activity table lists them with their premium so the small ones can be read as such. The 25-delta interpolation needs quotes either side of 0.25; expiries without them show no skew.
- **Earnings dates marked verify** are from the usual cadence; a company can move its date. FOMC dates for 2027 are the Fed's provisional calendar.
- **Only listed, kept lines.** The page keeps strikes 60-150% of spot and expiries within 400 days; LEAPS beyond that and far-out-of-the-money strikes are not in the ratios or GEX totals.
- **Dividends and rates** are not modelled in the ATM and skew figures (Cboe's IVs already carry them); the Strategy lab uses one flat rate and no dividends.
